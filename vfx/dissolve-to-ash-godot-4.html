Dissolve any mesh to ash or stone in Godot 4

Updated 2026-11-16. Tested on Godot 4.4.1 and 4.7.2, Forward+ and Compatibility.

Turning a mesh to ash in Godot 4 is one call to Cinelux.apply, passing the mesh and the style "ash". Change the style to "petrify" and the mesh turns to stone, then shatters. "acid" melts it into a puddle, and "dust" crumbles it to sand that blows away on the wind. The effect starts at the point you pass in. A hit on the chest makes the ash spread from the chest. The restore method on the CineluxDissolve3D node puts the original materials back. It works on any MeshInstance3D, including a walking character. This is CINELUX: 45 presets for death, dissolve and petrify effects, with shatter bursts, rising souls and reaper slashes, for Godot 4.4 and newer, without textures. The code and a demo you can run in the page are below.

The code

Cinelux.apply($Enemy/Mesh, "ash", hit_point)         # or "petrify", "acid", "dust"
var fx := CineluxDissolve3D.new()
$Statue/Mesh.add_child(fx)
fx.dissolve(hit_point)
fx.restore()                                        # puts the original materials back

Cinelux.apply is the one-line form. Keep a CineluxDissolve3D node around if you want its dissolved signal or need to restore the mesh later.

Try it in your browser

It loads the pack's own demo, about 8 MB the first time, and it opens on its first scene, “Ash from where it was hit”. Left and right arrow keys, or the on-screen buttons, move to other scenes. A desktop browser works best; the first start can take up to a minute while the shaders compile.

What is in the pack

There are also 2D sprite dissolves and a pixel-art mode. Rocks and crates burst into debris, souls lift off fallen bodies, and doom marks and miasma clouds fill out the set. I added a restore method so you can bring a mesh back.
